ABSTRACT
Stock price forecasting is hindered by the heteroscedastic nature of financial time series, in
whichturbulentandtranquilregimesalternate.AlthoughtheLongShort-TermMemory(LSTM)
network is widely used for this task, its gates are oblivious to such volatility variation, so

1. Introduction
Financial time series forecasting presents significant challenges due to the inherent nonlinear dynamics and
time-varying volatility of financial markets (Han et al., 2024). The increasing frequency of extreme events, such as
the COVID-19 pandemic and geopolitical conflicts, has heightened market uncertainty and underscored the need
for advanced computational methods that can adapt to rapidly changing market conditions. These methods must
capture key statistical characteristics while providing robust predictions to support critical financial decision-making.
The practical implications of accurate forecasting models are substantial, influencing global portfolio optimization,
derivativespricing,andsystemicriskassessment(Petrosinoetal.,2025).RecentadvancementsininterpretableAIfor
financeemphasizetheimportanceofbalancingpredictionaccuracywithmodelinterpretabilitytosupporthigh-stakes
decisions in investment banking, asset management, and regulatory supervision (Arsenault et al., 2025; Blasco et al.,
2024). This has led to the development of hybrid methods that combine the rigor of econometric models with the
pattern recognition capabilities of deep learning, creating a critical interdisciplinary focus in financial forecasting.

clustering and persistence (Alfeus et al., 2024; Kim and Won, 2018). However, their parametric structure imposes

The evolution of deep learning for time series forecasting has progressed from traditional statistical methods, such
as ARIMA, to recurrent neural networks, LSTMs, and Transformer architectures. Within this context, LSTMs retain

2. Related Work
In financial time series forecasting, traditional econometric models, particularly the GARCH family, have long
been used to capture the time-varying volatility clustering in asset returns. The classic GARCH(1,1) specification

patterns from high-frequency data. Sunet al. (2025) extended the dynamic conditional angularcorrelation framework
through GARCH-family variants, demonstrating superior portfolio optimization performance in markets with long
memorycharacteristics.Despitetheirstrongtheoreticalfoundationsandinterpretability,theseparametricframeworks
inherently assume fixed functional forms. Their prediction accuracy deteriorates sharply during structural breaks and
high-volatility periods, necessitating integration with more flexible, data-driven architectures.

3.2. Integrating Volatility into the LSTM Forgetting Gate
Financial time series are non-stationary and heteroskedastic. High volatility can appear without warning, and the
data distribution shifts over time. However, standard LSTM exhibits indiscriminate learning on such data (Sang and
Li, 2024). It treats every time step as equally learnable, leading to overfitting and prediction drift. To address this,
we propose a volatility-adaptive gating mechanism that makes the forget gate regime-aware. In turbulent periods, the
gateactivelysuppressesmemory retentionandforcesthenetworktoprioritize recentobservations.Incalmperiods,it
preserves long-term dependencies. This is achieved by embedding GARCH(1,1)-derived volatility estimates directly
into the forget gate via the Gsigmoid activation function.

We formulate this gating mechanism to address activation saturation and to support continuous value regression
through coupled dynamic regulation. Standard sigmoid activations tend to saturate when pre-activation inputs deviate
from typical ranges, which can cause gradient collapse and impede the fine-grained weight updates required for
numerical forecasting. The learnable parameter𝛾is introduced to adaptively adjust the activation slope, with the
intention of preserving gradient magnitude across varying input scales. Concurrently, the volatility scaling factor
𝜌𝑡 = 1∕(1 +𝜎 norm𝑡 +𝛿)multiplicatively bounds the gate output to prevent excessive memory retention during periods
of market stress. From a regression standpoint, this dual modulation is structured to establish a volatility conditioned
balance between prediction bias and variance. In stable market phases, higher𝜌𝑡 values encourage the preservation of
long term temporal patterns, which aims to reduce prediction variance and smooth baseline forecasts. In turbulent
phases, lower𝜌 𝑡 values accelerate memory turnover, prompting the network to prioritize recent information and
reduce systematic tracking lag. This design aligns the gating sensitivity with the heteroskedastic structure of stock
returns, seeking to prevent oversmoothing during tail events and overfitting to noise during calm phases. Ultimately,
this formulation is intended to promote stable gradient propagation and regime-adaptive memory decay, providing a
mechanistic foundation for continuous value approximation under varying market conditions.

behavior of the activation function. As shown in Figure 2, the Gsigmoid activation function adapts its response to

Beyond its performance advantages, the proposed Gsigmoid gating design offers inherent interpretability that is
absent in standard LSTM. In a conventional LSTM, the forget gate value is a learned function of the input and hidden
state, making its behavior opaque and difficult to attribute to specific market conditions. In Gsigmoid-LSTM, the
forget gate dynamics are directly tied to an economically meaningful variable, the estimated market volatility. When
the volatility estimate is high, the multiplicative scaling factor𝜌𝑡 automatically suppresses the forget gate output,
indicating that the model is actively discounting past information in response to market turbulence. Conversely, when
volatility is low,𝜌𝑡 approaches1, allowing the model to preserve long-term dependencies. Because𝜌𝑡 enters the gate
only as a multiplicative amplitude factor, the gate output can be viewed as a volatility-driven scaling of a data-driven
sigmoid response. The scaling is a monotone and directly traceable function of an observable market indicator, while
the underlying response still depends on the learned pre-activation. This decomposition makes the gating dynamics
