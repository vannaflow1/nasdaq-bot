Put-Call Parity Violations and Market Frictions in 
China's SSE 50 ETF Options Market: Evidence from the 
AI Boom of 2023 

Yijia Sun* 
Faculty of Arts & Humanities, University College London, London, UK 
*Corresponding author: Yijia Sun. 

Abstract 
This paper empirically investigates the deviation of the put - call parity (PCP) in China's SSE 50ETF options 
market (510050 XSHG) for the whole year of 2023-when the release of ChatGPT triggered an unprecedented 
investor sentiment hype of artificial intelligence. Based on 15,604 pairs of matched call -put options covering 
all 242 trading days, we detected that there was always a systematic and persistent overpricing of calls over 
put options. We interpret this phenomenon as follows: The explosive market dem and by retail investors on 
call options -a leveraged sentiment on AI -related market bullishness -out-weighted the arbitrage capacity of 
constrained arbitrageurs by market frictions to make corrections to the mispricing through a reverse arbitrage 
strategy (selling call options, buying put options, and buying spot options). Using quarterly dummy variables 
as proxies for AI market sentiment cycle, we found that by controlling time stage variables, the R² rose from 
8.9% to 71.67% to confirm that demand dynamics driven by market sentiment rather than the specific features 
of contracts-are the primary drivers for the variability in the PCP violation phenomena. Compared with the 
baseline of AI craze hype in the first quarter, the quarterly effect shows monotonically decreasing, while the 
implied residuals bias the fourth quarter, which is still a positive one. Confirmed is that the permanent structural 
friction lower limit will persist even after the sentiment premium of AI fades. Our research result provides 
fresh evidence in terms of behavioral demand shocks' interaction with the institutional friction on in emerging 
markets, thereby creating option price biases.

put-call parity, option mispricing, SSE 50 ETF options, investor sentiment, market frictions 

1. Introduction 
Put-call parity (PCP) represents an important fundamental no-arbitrage condition in finance economics [1]. 
In a frictionless market, the equality c − p = S0 − Ke−rT must always be fulfilled strictlyly: if DEV is positive 
(DEV > 0) call options are overpriced with respect to puts, while for negative (DEV < 0) put options are 
overpriced and the vice versa. In either case, arbitrageurs should be capable to arbitrage awa y the mispricing.

In fact, market frictions (transaction cost, capital constraint, and institutional limitations) can lead to 
sustainable deviation within a band around the theoretical parity. 
China's securities options market provides an interesting and illustrative case in point. The SSE 50 ETF 
options market (510050 XSHG) that launched in 2015, has been operating under a regulatory regime that limits 
securities lending, requires high margin r eserves on writing options, and features a large domestic retail 
investor population characterized by strong directional biases. All these features set into the conditions for 
persistent and economically meaningful violations of PCP. 
The year of 2023 added a new strong factor for behavioral dimensions: the AI -driven euphoric frenzy 
sparked by the launch of ChatGPT in late 2022 has spawned an unprecedented demand by retail investors to 
use call options as a leveraged tool to express bullish views about AI-related technologies. Our main hypothesis 
is that this explosion in demand for calls pushed the prices of calls consistently over their PCP prices, leading 
to positive (DEV > 0) or DEV (i.e., call overpricing) in this case. This effect is most marked in Q1 2 02 3 - 
when AI sentiment has been at its peak - but gradually fades throughout Q4s. 
2. Literature Review 
2.1 Put-Call Parity: Theory and Empirical Evidence 
Standard PCP is the result of the application of the law of one price to a portfolio consisting of a portfolio 
of options and the underlying stock asset [1]. With the presence of transaction costs in the market, the identity 
becomes a no arbitrage band condition [2, 3]. For mature markets, Kamara and Miller [4] and Nisbet [5] find 
that most of observed PCP violations disappear once bid -ask spreads and commissions have been accounted 
for. For China's options of the SSE 50 ETF market (510050 XSHG), Xia [6] offers a gene ral four-element 
analysis of PCP deviations, and identifies that bid-ask spreads, brokerage fees, the rate of financing spread and 
option writing costs are the key friction elements in the system. 
2.2 Call Overpricing, Retail Demand, and Option Writing Constraint 
For the case of DEV > , call options are overpriced relative to puts. The theoretical arbitrage strategy that 
should eliminate this discrepancy is: sell overpriced calls, buy underpriced puts, buy the underlying spot, and 
borrow the present value of the strike price. This strate gy does not involve a short sale of the underlying, but 
it does involve writing (selling) call options, an action that is heavily constrained in China's options market. 
Writing an option requires a deposit of substantial margin guarantee, often on the orde r of 10 –20% of the 
underlying value in addition to the option option option premium, which makes it a capital -intensive scale of 
massive arbitrages feasible. Han [7] and Bollen and Whaley [8] prove that directional demand (with for specific 
options e.g., calls), particularly retail investor demand for calls, can resu lt in persistent options mispricing 
when arbitrage capacity of professional intermediaries is limited by their capital constraints. 
The link between retail sentiment and call overpricing is well established. Garleanu, Pedersen, and 
Poteshman [9] show that end-user demand for options is an important pricedetermining factor in option other 
than the Black Scholes model, and that retail investors net call -buying pressure results in an upward bias of 
implied volatility in calls. In the China where retail investor investors account for more than 70% of the total 
volume of option options trading, this channel is particularly intense. 
2.3 Short-Selling Limitation: Indirect Impact to Call over Pricing 
While the restriction on short selling primarily operates as a mechanism that restricts arbitrageurs by 
eliminating call over pricing (DEV > ) , it has a secondary effect of amplifying call over pricing, via the delta 
hedging channel. Market makers who write (sell) call have to delta hedge by buying the underlying ETF, but 
they cannot effectively manage their inventory risk if they are restricted from being able to short the ETF. The 
resulting limited hedging capacity forces market makers to charge a higher premium to their writing, resulting 
further driving c up, thereby increasing DEV > . This transmission channel is formal in [ Diamond and 
Verrecchia [10], and Li, Du, and Hu [11] document the empirical relevance of the channel in Chinese equity 
options markets.

2.4 Investor Sentiment and the AI Boom 
Baker and Wurgler [12] establish that market investors' sentiment has an impact on asset price independent 
of their fundamentals. Da, Engelberg, and Gao [13] develop internet search intensity as a proxy for retail 
attention, documenting that this variable can predict short-term short-pressure on stock prices. The context of 
Chinese options market, Fang, Wen, and Zhang [14] document the prediction effect of Baidu search intensity 
on the asymmetries of the implied volatility. But daily search index is subject to high frequency noises, key 
selection bias and concerns for manipulation. We follow the lit erature of macro sentiment [11, 15] that using 
quarterly dummies as regimes proxies, which will eliminate noise of idiosyncratic daily variations, while 
maintaining the macro phase structure of AI sentiment cycles with economical meaningfulness. 
There has been previous literature for frictional structures [6], retail demand [7, 9], and short selling 
constraint [10]; The 3 dimensions [11] respectively explained the reasons for option pricing bias, but these 3 
have not been added into the unified system, lack of identification and measurement of how event -driven 
emotional shocks synergistically amplify bias on the existing institutional frictions. There are 3 contributions 
of this article. Firstly we demonstrated PCP bias of SSE 50 ETF Options market in 2023 is consistent positive, 
all 15604 observations generated DEV >. Which indicates that the systematic pricing of call options is too high 
compared to put option, which is consistent with AI -driven call option speculative demand. Secondly we 
introduced quarterly dummy variables as a tool of artificial intelligence sentiment cycle, avoiding noise of 
daily search index and key words biased, and clearly identified the macro stage shift from optimism in the 1st 
quarter and normalization in the 4th quarter. This design elevated R² value from 8.9% to 71.67% indicated that 
the time variation drivers by emotion is the primary driving factor. Thirdly, we demonstrated the option writing 
constraint - the major friction preventing calls' pricing correction - has formed a structure of bottom at around 
0.1241 yuan, which will exist after AI sentiment premium fades. These contributions filled the aforementioned 
research gap. Chapter 3 builds a theoretical framework that involves the lower limit structural friction and the 
component of emotional premium based on that, providing a hypothetical base of empirical analysis. 
3. Theoretical Framework 
3.1 Put-Call Parity and the Sign of Deviations 
For European options on a non-dividend-paying underlying, PCP requires: 
 c − p = S0 − Ke−rT (1) 
Note: SSE 50 ETF generates minor annual cash dividends. This paper ignores dividend yields for simplicity, 
and the resulting bias toward PCP deviations is empirically negligible. 
We define the raw PCP deviation as: 
 DEV = c − p − (S0 − Ke−rT) (2)

3.2 No-Arbitrage Band under Option Writing Constraints 
Following the asymmetric friction framework of Jouini and Kallal [3], the no-arbitrage condition becomes 
a band:

 [−Clower, + Cupper] (3) 
where C_lower incorporates the cost of the forward arbitrage (correcting DEV < 0, being limited by short-selling 
costs), and C_upper incorporates the cost of the reverse arbitrage (correcting DEV > 0, being limited by option 
writing margin and transaction costs). C_upper widens the larger the writing margin requirements for calls are–as 
is usually the case with China exchanges requiring 10–20% of the underlying value of calls as naked call writing 
collateral–allowing larger positive deviations to persist without activating a profit-reversing reverse arbitrage. 
Restricted short selling also indirectly widens C_upper through the market maker channel: the restricted 
delta-hedging capacity limits the premium market makers charge to write calls, increasing c and widening the 
observed DEV. These option writing margin, transaction, and market -maker delta-hedging premium together 
create a structural no-arbitrage floor DEV > 0, which cannot be arbitraged away. 
3.3 The AI Sentiment Amplification Mechanism 
We interpret the observed deviation as two components: 
 DEVt = Floor + Sentimentt + εt (4) 
where Floor indicates the cost of the underlying friction (option writing constraints + delta -hedging 
premium), which creates a permanent overpricing, and Sentimentt captures the further deviation from call 
demand in retail AI sentiment. In Q1 2023, the height of the ChatGPT -induced euphoria, rampant retail call 
demand significantly inflated Sentimentt . As AI sentiment normalized during Q2–4, Sentimentt continuously 
dropped, letting DEV drop to approach Floor. Therefore, this framework offers three testable predictions: (i) 
DEV > 0 for the whole sample; (ii) DEV has its maximum Q1 and continuously decreases; (iii) Q4 still shows 
substantial structural floor presence. 
4. Data and Methodology 
4.1 Data Sources and Sample Construction 
Our sample includes the daily closing prices of all SSE 50 ETF options (510050 XSHG) traded on Shanghai 
Stock Exchange for entire 2023. Options prices are extracted from JoinQuant's OPT_DAILY_PRICE database. 
SSE ETF 50 closing prices and fixed annualized risk-free rate of 2% (comparable with prevailing three-month 
Treasury bill) are used to derive PCP benchmarks. 
We construct matched call-put pairs by restricting to identical strike K and expiry date on the same day.

7. Conclusion 
This paper empirically examines the put-call parity deviations in the Chinese SSE 50 ETF options market 
in 2023, using the AI sentiment wave generated by ChatGPT as a natural identification strategy. Three major 
results are achieved. First, 2023 is a year where call options are systematically and persistently overpriced 
relative to puts, with not a single observation giving DEV ≤ 0. Second, the inclusion of quarterly dummy 
variables as proxies for the AI sentiment cycle raises R2 from 8.9% to 71.67%, indicating that the main source 
of PCP deviation dynamics is sentiment -driven time variation. Third, the structural decomposition uncovers 
that 72.1% of the peak Q1 deviation is a permanent friction floor due to option writing constraints and market-
maker hedging costs, while 27.9% is the transient AI sentiment premium that dissipates by Q4. 
So, we are inspired by the above findings. Even in a highly regulated environment (including Option 
Writing Constraints and Market-maker Delta-hedging Premiums) call options are still overvalued and obvious 
PCP deviations are still present even if AI sentiment completely fades (Q4). If the regulators want to reduce 
the deviations in the options market, they should not focus on the sentiment of retail investors. Instead, they 
should appropriately reduce the margin ratio for option sellers or relax restrictions on ETF short selling, so 
that arbitrageurs can implement the “sell call, buy put” strategy at a lower cost and smooth out deviations, 
especially during special periods affected by shocks (AI) and sentiment. If a call option is purchased in Q1 
2023, a sentiment premium of 0.0502 yuan must be borne. For the average retail investor, when a highly 
innovative technological concept is already quite well known (Q1), it is best to stay calm as the pur chase of 
call options at this point would entail an extra sentiment premium. It would be more cost effective to sell call 
options at this point or wait for sentiment to subside (Q4) to buy back. For quantitative researchers, the 
quarterly decomposition met hod mentioned in the text can be used to predict the size of the price deviation 
through the sentiment cycle, and adjust the strategy in advance. In the meantime, in the Chinese SSE 50ETF 
options market, the existence of the price floor of 0.12 yuan, the theoretical no -arbitrage range should be not 
set at 0 when researchers carry out arbitrage strategies but 0.12. Ar bitrage is only profitable when the price 
deviation is much higher than 0.12 yuan. Several limitations need to be acknowledged. The transaction cost 
measure is approximate; a more accurate friction decomposition would be possible with direct bid-ask spread 
data. We assume the risk-free rate is fixed, but robustness checks show little sensitivity to this assumption. The
