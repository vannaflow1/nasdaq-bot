Multiple Stopping and Exercise Boundaries
for American Put Options
Katsunori Ano
October 22, 2026
Abstract
This article studies a finite-horizon American put option with up tomexercise rights
under the risk-neutral Black–Scholes model, where consecutive exercises must be separated
by a prescribed refractory periodδ >0 and unused rights may expire. For each number
of remaining rights, we show that optimal exercise is characterized by a single exercise
boundary and that these boundaries are ordered by the number of remaining rights. We
prove optimality of the first boundary-hitting time and establish local Lipschitz continuity
of the exercise boundaries within each open deadline strip. Away from the deadlines, we
derive smooth fit, the associated quasi-variational inequality and free-boundary formulation,
an early-exercise-premium representation, recursive Volterra-type boundary equations, and
a verification theorem. We also extend the recursive formulation to an independent random
termination time, thereby allowing for exogenous termination of the option contract.
Keywords:multiple stopping; American put option; single-crossing; free-boundary problem;
quasi-variational inequality; random termination.

1 Introduction
The finite-horizon American put is a classical example of an optimal stopping problem; see,
for example, Jacka [13], Lamberton and Lapeyre [14], Merton [15], Peskir and Shiryaev [18].
The purpose of this paper is to develop a corresponding optimal-boundary analysis for the
multiple-exercise (multiple-stopping) setting, characterizing the optimal exercise rule when the
holder has several exercise rights and successive exercises must be separated by a deterministic
refractory period.
The general multiple-stopping framework is well established. The recursive reduction of a
multiple-stopping problem to a sequence of single-stopping problems already appears in clas-
sical discrete-time theory; see Haggstrom [12] and the historical discussion in Bender [5]. A
systematic account of discrete-time multiple stopping problems and their applications is given
in the monograph by Sofronov and Szajowski [21]. For a Japanese-language introduction to op-
timal stopping problems that also treats multiple stopping, see Ano [1]. Carmona and Touzi [8]
proved existence and recursive construction of multiple-exercise rules in a broad Snell-envelope
setting and, in the Black–Scholes model, gave a constructive perpetual solution together with a
numerical approximation procedure for finite maturity. Related potential-theoretic methods for
linear diffusions were developed by Carmona and Dayanik [7]. For finite-horizon problems with
a deterministic refractory period, Bender [5] established a continuous-time reduction principle
and a Doob–Meyer-type decomposition allowing for right-hand discontinuities of the multiple-
exercise value process. For another class of finite-horizon optimal stopping problems arising in
mathematical finance, formulated for exponential L´ evy processes, see Ano and Ivanov [2]. Our
question is more specific: what can be proved analytically about the geometry and regularity

of the finite-horizon exercise region for an American put when the exercise rights are optional
and successive exercises are separated by a fixed refractory period?
A closely related finite-horizon swing-put problem was analyzed by De Angelis and Ki-
tapbayev [10], where all rights must be exercised before maturity. That obligation can make
exercise optimal even when the put payoff is zero and leads, for each right except the last, to
a stopping region bounded by two time-dependent curves. Here unused rights may expire, so
there is no contractual reason to exercise an out-of-the-money right merely to preserve later op-
portunities. For every finite number of rights we prove, under the standing model assumptions,
that the stopping region is a single lower interval determined by one exercise boundary.
This paper develops a continuous-time counterpart of the discrete-time framework in Ano
[3], replacing the geometric random walk by the Black–Scholes diffusion while retaining the
multiple-exercise and refractory-period structure. That companion study considers American
put, Russian, and floating-strike geometric-average Asian contracts, as well as independent
random maturity. Broader Japanese-language lecture notes covering the surrounding optimal
multiple-stopping and mathematical finance framework are Ano [4]. The present article de-
velops in English, and in research-article form, the material of Chapters 3–5 of those notes,
reorganized around the one-right American put, the multiple-exercise American put with re-
fractory periods, and the extension to random termination, and supplemented by several new
proofs developed for the present article. The presentation here is self-contained.
Finite maturity creates another important feature of the multiple-exercise problem. Because
two successive exercises must be separated by the refractory periodδ, there are deterministic
times at which one of the remaining exercise opportunities ceases to be usable. Define
dj :=T−jδ, j= 0,1,2, . . . , d 0 =T.
At timed j, there is still just enough time to accommodate one additional exercise after waiting
for the required refractory period, whereas immediately afterd j there is not. We call these
timesdeterministic feasibility deadlines. They divide the time horizon into strips on which only
a certain number of exercise rights can still be used before maturity, so the original multiple-
right problem reduces to the corresponding lower-right problem. When a deadline is crossed
from left to right, the effective number of usable rights drops by one. As a consequence,
the value function may have a downward right-hand jump at a deadline, and the optimal
exercise boundary may jump as well. Right-hand discontinuities of the value process at the
timesT−jδare already treated in the general framework of Bender [5]. Here we study their
implications for the exercise boundaries and the associated transmission conditions. Away
from the deadlines, the standard quasi-variational inequality (QVI) and smooth-fit relations
are recovered below from the standing Black–Scholes assumptions. At a deadline, however, the
usual time-continuous matching is replaced by a max-type transmission condition reflecting
the loss of one effective exercise right.
The main contribution of this paper is the single-crossing analysis and the resulting one-
sided exercise boundaries, ordered by the number of remaining rights, for the optional multiple-
exercise American put, with the feasibility-deadline structure kept explicit. We first establish
the recursive single-stopping representation, stagewise reduction across deadline strips, and
basic monotonicity, convexity, Lipschitz, and stripwise time-regularity properties of the value
functions. We then combine a Bermudan approximation with themarginal-right values, which
serve as a key comparison device in the present analysis and are also developed in the companion
discrete-time study Ano [3]. The standard Bermudan approach restricts exercise to a finite time
grid and studies convergence as the mesh tends to zero; see Bouchard and Warin [6, Section 2.2]
and Pag` es [16, Chapter 11] for general accounts. For the present multiple-exercise problem,
we instead use deadline-aligned grids withh=δ/Nthat contain maturity and every relevant
feasibility deadline, so that the refractory period is represented exactly byNsteps. This

alignment is essential for the backward-induction argument, because an unaligned recursion
can cross a time at which the effective number of usable rights changes.
The aligned scheme yields a slope bound for the marginal-right valuef [m]δ , defined later for
them-right problem, and hence asingle-crossing property: the excess valuef [m]δ (t, x)−(K−x)+
is nondecreasing on (0, K), so its zero set is a lower interval. This single-crossing property is the
key structural step in the boundary analysis. Together with the strict waiting-loss argument,
which excludes optimal exercise at or above the strike, it yields a one-sided stopping region for
every finite number of rights. Thus we obtain the threshold characterization
V [m](t, x) =g[m]δ (t, x)⇐ ⇒f[m]δ (t, x) = (K−x)+ ⇐ ⇒x≤b[m](t),
whereV [m] denotes the value function of them-right problem,g [m]δ is the stopping reward, and
b[m] is the exercise boundary. The comparison of marginal-right values across exercise levels
also yields the ordering of the exercise boundaries by the number of remaining rights, and we
prove that the first boundary-hitting time is optimal.
The remaining regularity issue is resolved by a localized level-set argument in log-price
coordinates, adapted from the probabilistic method of De Angelis and Stabile [11] and combined
with the weak Duhamel representation proved earlier in the paper. This yields local Lipschitz
continuity of every exercise boundary on each open deadline strip and closes the classical
delayed-value regularity recursively at every finite level. Smooth fit then follows away from
deterministic deadlines. The classical QVI relations in the open stopping and continuation
regions, together with the associated free-boundary formulation, consequently hold under the
standing Black–Scholes assumptions. For the early-exercise-premium representation we use the
weak Duhamel formula directly, rather than a local-time-space formula; no additional boundary
monotonicity or continuation-side boundary regularity is required. This leads to a recursive
Volterra-type integral equation for the exercise boundary in which the deadline jumps enter
explicitly, extending the classical one-right integral-equation characterization.
Finally, we extend the recursive architecture to an independent random termination time.
A general conditional survival function weights future rewards, and under a multiplicative
survival condition the survival factors combine consistently along the recursion. Exponential
termination with intensityλis equivalent, for valuation purposes, to replacing the discount
raterbyr+λin the stopping functional, while the risk-neutral drift of the underlying remains
r; in this case we prove that the recursive and original formulations coincide. For a general
survival law, a complete proof of this equivalence is not pursued here.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the Black–Scholes
model and the one-right American put. Sections 3–5 develop the recursive multiple-stopping
problem, the deadline structure, marginal-right values, the single-crossing property, and the
optimal boundary. Section 6 gives the QVI and free-boundary formulations, the verification
result, the early-exercise premium representation, and recursive Volterra equations for the exer-
cise boundary. Section 7 treats random termination, Section 8 reports numerical illustrations.
