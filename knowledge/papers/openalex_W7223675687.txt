Abstract.The study analyzes volatility regimes, tail risk and machine learning forecastability of seven financial assets
(BTC, ETH, the S&P 500, NASDAQ, Gold, Crude Oil, and US Dollar Index) from 2017 to 2026. Structural break analysis
shows that financial instability is characterized by variance, not by a change in the mean. EGARCH is preferred in five
out of seven assets, with significant leverage effects in the equity indices. According to EVT analysis, ETH and BTC are
the highest-tail-risk assets, while Crude Oil has the heaviest tail. Kupiec and Christoffersen VaR back testing results
show that Gold’ s actual exceedance rate at the 95th percentile is almost twice the expected exceedance rate. XGBoost
doesn’ t statistically outperform Linear Regression (DM=−0.403, p=0.687), and the most important predictive element
is a 5-day rolling mean return. The results show that most predictive information in daily returns is contained in linear
autoregressive features.
1. Introduction
It is still an empirical issue whether nonlinear ML models offer better forecasts of returns than
well-specified linear models, a question that has clear consequences for risk modeling, regulation,
and the use of ML in finance. Another question to address is whether risk arises mostly from
changes in the expected return or structural breaks in the conditional variance in the financial
markets. This paper attempts to answer both questions based on a multi-asset panel dataset of
daily returns from 2017 to 2026.
The empirical procedure consists of a combination of strict temporal out-of-sample validation,
Diebold–Mariano test [3] with the Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold correction [8] and Newey–West
HAC standard errors [20], and SHAP decomposition to decompose predictive contributions at the
level of features. The key finding is a straightforward negative one: XGBoost does not improve
upon Linear Regression in predicting daily returns of BTC (DM=−0.403, p-value=0.687), and this

(ii) ML capacity limitations – empirical proof: DM test proves that XGBoost is not statistically

2. Data, ReturnConstruction,andValidationProtocol
2.1.Data.Closing prices on a daily basis of seven assets are collected from Yahoo Finance through
the usage of R software, version 4.3.2, and the quantmod package from January 2017 till May 2026
(approximately 2,320 business days). The data set includes BTC-USD, ETH-USD, S&P 500 (ˆGSPC),
NASDAQ Composite (ˆIXIC), Gold spot (GC=F), Crude Oil WTI (CL=F), and the US Dollar Index
(DX-Y.NYB). The log-returns of each asset are calculated using the formula
rt =ln(P t/Pt−1)(2.1)
whereP t is the closing price on dayt. The data covers several stress events, including the crypto
bear market in 2018, the COVID-19 crash in March 2020, the crypto bull market in 2020-2021, the
FTX crash in November 2022.

2.2.Leakage Prevention and Temporal Validation.All features are generated following a rigor-
ous causal protocol: rolling features use only past observations up to the forecast date; lagged
features use observations at t−k to make forecasts at t; and feature scaling is done only within the
training period, with test set observations being scaled based on training period statistics. The
temporal train-test split follows a strict split of first 70% training and last 30% test, with no shuffling
or look-back. Three tests for validation include: (a) Ljung-Box test for model residuals giving p
=0.237, indicating no residual autocorrelation; (b) subperiod directional accuracy for each year
(2024: 70.7%; 2025: 76.8%; 2026: 79.6%) leading to a time-weighted average of ~75.8%, consistent
with the 76.24% figure reported in Table 4; and (c) a permutation test reducing directional accuracy
to ~50%, indicating no leakage. The forecasting problem requires only the prediction of direction
of change in returns and not the magnitude.
Rolling feature overlap: The rolling window predictors (roll_mean_5), which share observations
between adjacent dates, have the potential to create local autocorrelation in the signal for predic-
tions. The annual subperiods and permutation test provide evidence that our primary results do
not arise from this type of structure.
HAR-RV exclusion: HAR-RV specifications need the realised volatility data at shorter intervals
than daily, which is not available in this dataset. Elastic Net and AR(1) are good benchmark
comparators within the daily return setting.
3. StatisticalDiagnostics
For each of the seven assets, the ADF tests reject the null hypothesis of a unit root for log-returns
(p≤0.01). The tests for levels at KPSS at level 1 and 2 give p values below 0.01, which is used as
the rejection limit, and the null hypothesis of stationarity is rejected. This result is consistent with
the test results for log-returns, which show that log-returns are stationary. The Ljung-Box tests are
used to check any significant serial correlation in most return series, approaching the martingale
difference hypothesis (p=0.188) for DXY. The results of the ARCH-LM tests are important for all
assets (p<10 −8), showing conditional heteroskedasticity in all the test periods. All assets fail to
meet normality criteria at the Jarque-Bera (p≈0) and OIL has the greatest excess kurtosis (26.51),
while the lowest is found in DXY (4.68). The results are fully reported in table 1.

5.3.VaR Backtesting.The underestimation of VaR with respect to gold is one of the most practical
insights gained from this study, with 8.90% realizations above the 95th percentile against an
expected 5.00% (Kupiec [12] test, p<0.001), and 4.92% exceedances above the 99th percentile
compared to an expected 1.00% (Kupiec p<0.001). The failure of historical VaR to account for tail
exposure is attributed to the fact that extreme losses occur as a result of sudden shifts in monetary
policies and demand for safety, which are not adequately captured in the historical estimation
period. The asset with the best calibration is Crude Oil (Kupiec p=0.762), while BTC and ETH

6. MachineLearningForecasting, ForecastEvaluation,andEconomicValue
6.1.Scope, Models, and Features.Asset scope: The ML analysis targets BTC return forecasting.
BTC was selected for its combination of unconditional volatility (daily SD: 4.185%), documented
autocorrelation structure (Ljung-Box p=0.025), and sufficient exchange liquidity for a directional
simulation. It represents a demanding environment for testing the limits of predictive gains from
nonlinear models.
Supplementary results for ETH and SP500 yield the same qualitative conclusion: XGBoost does
not provide a statistically significant improvement over Linear Regression (DM p≈0.70 and p≈
0.65, respectively), suggesting that the main finding is not unique to BTC.
Six models are compared: (1) Naïve benchmark (random walk); (2) AR(1); (3) Elastic Net
(penalty cross-validated within training window only); (4) Linear Regression (OLS); (5) Random
Forest; (6) XGBoost. The feature set for models (3)–(6) includes BTC lags at 1, 2, 3, 5, and 10 days;
five-day rolling mean and standard deviation of BTC returns; and one-period lagged returns from
ETH, SP500, NASDAQ, GOLD, OIL, and DXY. All estimation, scaling, and cross-validation uses
training data exclusively.
Multiple comparisons: The main conclusions — no significant difference between Linear Re-
gression and XGBoost (p=0.687), or between Linear Regression and Elastic Net (p=0.754)
— are robust to standard Bonferroni correction and are not marginal rejections that depend on
multiplicity adjustment.
6.2.Point Forecast Performance.Table 4 Panel A reports out-of-sample accuracy metrics. All
supervised models achieve directional accuracy well above 70%, substantially outperforming the
naïve random-walk benchmark (47.53%). Linear Regression achieves the highest directional ac-
curacy (76.24%) and the lowest RMSE (0.02034) and MAE (0.01541). XGBoost achieves marginally
higher RMSE (0.02057) and lower directional accuracy (72.47%) than Linear Regression, while
Random Forest trails both structured models with RMSE=0.02206 and DA=71.29%.
6.3.Diebold-Mariano Test Interpretation.All structured models significantly outperform the
naïve benchmark, with p<10−9 for all (Table 4 Panel B). However, the only meaningful comparisons
are between Linear Regression, Elastic Net and XGBoost, where none of the pairwise DM tests
reject the null of equal predictive accuracy (p=0.687 and p=0.754 respectively). The linear
functional form is significantly better than the AR(1) model (p<0.001), and the cross-market lags
and rolling statistics contain significant predictive information beyond the information contained
in a single autoregressive lag. Random Forest is the weakest structured model, and it performs
significantly worse than Linear Regression (p=0.008) and XGBoost (p=0.029), probably because
of the loss of sensitivity to extreme returns, which are the ones making up the strong momentum
signal.

6.4.SHAP Feature Importance.Table 5 and Figure 4 show SHAP analysis for the XGBoost model.

as the most important information contained in the variables is linear in nature and non-linear
relationships between variables are of little importance.
6.5.Economic Value of Forecasts.Execution note: The simulation in Table 6 is based on an ideal
execution at daily closing prices without any bid-ask spreads, slippages, market impacts, margin
calls, or size limitations. Without transaction costs, the Sharpe ratio is 6.75 for the Linear Strategy
and 6.22 for the XGBoost Strategy. The numbers generated are upper-bound estimates of the
economic potential of the statistical advantage. The period used for evaluation (September 2024
– May 2026, n≈425 days, ~1.7 years) covers a time when BTC momentum was mostly favorable,
and thus tends to overestimate annualized performance.
Table 6 reports transaction-cost sensitivity for the two main forecasting models. Even under
a 1.00% per-trade cost assumption, both strategies retain positive annualized returns, although
Sharpe ratios decline substantially. This confirms that the directional signal is not erased imme-
diately by severe execution frictions, while also showing that the high frictionless Sharpe ratios
should be interpreted cautiously.

8. VolatilityRegimeShifts andDynamicDependence
8.1.Structural Break Analysis.Structural breaks are identified based on the PELT algorithm [11],
wherein the BIC penalty is imposed individually on the conditional mean and conditional variance
of each returns’ time series. The findings show an interesting and consistent result, whereby there
are zero mean changepoints for all the seven assets analyzed, while there are five to eleven variance
changepoints per asset. The total lack of mean changepoints coupled with high variance instability
proves the assertion that financial instability in this particular sample is driven by changes in risk
intensity, and not change in the direction of return expectation.
Structural breaks detection identifies zero mean changepoints but 5-11 variance changepoints
per asset (BTC 10, ETH 9, SP500 11, NASDAQ 11, GOLD 7, OIL 8, DXY 5).
SP500 and NASDAQ have the highest number of changepoints (11 for both indices), which
indicates that they are highly sensitive to shifts in Federal Reserve monetary policy stance and
macroeconomic shocks. BTC has 10 variance changepoints from 2018 to 2022 but has much fewer
changes afterwards, corroborating the hypothesis that there is less structural variation in post-
2023 BTC variance regimes. The structure of variance regimes corresponds with the findings
from SHAP analysis: if expected returns are approximately constant, then roll_mean_5 reflects
momentum within variance regimes.

seven-asset system. The dynamic correlation equation

on conditional correlation behavior. First, the March 2020 crash associated with COVID-19 caused
the largest cross-asset deleveraging event in the sample: conditional correlations between BTC
and SP500 shot up from close to zero to roughly 0.60 in two weeks, driven by margin calls and
liquidity requirements that led to a simultaneous liquidation of assets. Second, the November
2022 FTX failure was largely crypto-specific: conditional correlations between BTC and SP500 and

9. Discussion andLimitations
9.1.Predictability Constraints and Regulatory Implications.The findings of the DM test confirm
that the improvement in forecast accuracy gained through the use of nonlinear ML models is
statistically insignificant in this case of daily return forecasts. There is no rejection of the null
hypothesis of equal forecast accuracy between Linear Regression and XGBoost (DM=−0.403, p
=0.687) and Linear Regression and Elastic Net (p=0.754). Most importantly, Linear Regression
significantly outperforms the AR(1) model (p<0.001), which means that the lags across different
markets and the rolling statistics contain information that can be used for prediction, and it is
linear information.
This evidence reinforces the focus on model parsimony since there are no observable statistical
or economic benefits from increased complexity. In terms of practical application, organizations
that are exposed to returns of BTC can apply the simple yet effective linear models for predicting
the daily point without any loss in terms of accuracy of forecasting. Economic value simulation
further supports this assertion since the difference in the Sharpe ratio for the Linear Strategy (6.75)
and the XGBoost Strategy (6.22) is minimal and remains small in different cost regimes, similar to
the results of the Diebold-Mariano test that showed equal predictive accuracy of both models.
9.2.Limitations.Asset scope: The main analysis is confined to BTC. Predictive performance
could vary for smaller-cap assets with lower liquidity, different autocorrelation structures, or
higher levels of nonlinearity.
Sample specification: This study covers unusual times marked by extreme shocks such as the
COVID-19 pandemic, the FTX debacle, and aggressive monetary policy tightening. The predictive
structure uncovered for daily returns might not hold for low-volatility environments or structural
breaks in crypto markets’ microstructure.
Regime-conditional performance: ML performance is not assessed conditionally on volatility
regime, which is crucial given the 5–11 variance changepoints found for each asset. Nonlinear
models may perform better in certain regimes despite their lack of an unconditional advantage.
Execution assumptions: The trading simulation assumes perfect execution at daily closing
prices. For large-scale portfolios, transaction costs including bid-ask spreads, slippage, and market
impact, which vary for BTC depending on the exchange, would reduce profits significantly.
Alternative data and intraday frequency: The results are based on daily returns from closing
prices with no other predictors than prices themselves. Nonlinear models could improve returns

using alternative data or intraday frequencies, as well as by introducing order book, sentiment, or
news information.
Deep learning: LSTM and Transformer networks are not considered in this study. Since linear
momentum signals dominate in SHAP and the sample is relatively short (2,320 observations), the
role of deep learning methods in this particular context is likely to be negligible; comparing both
approaches will be left for future research.
10. Conclusion
This paper has assessed the volatility regimes, tail risk, and limitations of machine learning
in daily return forecasting using seven financial assets during 2017–2026. The following four
empirical findings summarize the main results of the paper.
First, the structural break analysis indicates no mean changepoint, while 5–11 variance change-
points per asset are observed, suggesting that the financial market instability is more of a variance
change in the intensity of the risks that were undertaken, rather than a change in the direction of
the expected returns. Second, EGARCH specifications perform better in 5 out of 7 assets (Table
2), with significant leverage effects in the equity markets but not in cryptocurrencies. Third, in
conjunction with the backtests of the VaR models of Kupiec [12] and Christoffersen [2] (Table 3),
historical VaR is shown to systematically underestimate the tail risk of the Gold of both the 95%
and 99% levels, which have direct implications for the allocation of regulatory capital and internal
risk management.
Fourth, and most directly relevant to the recent literature on ML in finance: the DM test shows
that XGBoost is statistically indistinguishable from Linear Regression, both across subperiods
of the year, as well as across validation schemes (Table 4). The main predictive contribution
(mean|SHAP| =0.02339) comes from a five-day rolling mean return, a linear momentum signal
(Table 5). Based on the frictionless economic value simulation, the Linear Strategy (Sharpe 6.75,
max drawdown−3.96%) performed in similar fashion to XGBoost Strategy (Sharpe 6.22, max
drawdown−6.14%), which is consistent with the DM’ s result. The results indicate that most of the
information contained in the daily returns of BTC is captured by relatively simple, short memory
linear features, and that complexity in the nonlinear models offers very little additional value.
Priority extensions include: (i) regime-conditional ML performance evaluation of how variance
changepoints influence the accuracy of the model; (ii) data from intraday: there is a possibility that
the nonlinearity of microstructure and order flow may bring an advantage to the nonlinear models
over the linear ones in this data; (iii) deep learning comparisons with the formally established
benchmarks; and (iv) alternative data integration to see if the use of cross-sectional or textual
features affects the linear versus nonlinear balance.
