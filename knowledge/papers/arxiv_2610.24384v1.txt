 Title: A decomposition-integration framework coupling singular spectrum analysis with a volatility-adaptive LSTM for stock price forecasting 
 Authors: Xin-Rui Lu , Da-Qing Zhang View a PDF of the paper titled A decomposition-integration framework coupling singular spectrum analysis with a volatility-adaptive LSTM for stock price forecasting, by Xin-Rui Lu and 1 other authors 
 View PDF 
 HTML (experimental) 

 Abstract: Financial time series exhibit multi-scale, high-noise, and non-stationary volatility dynamics. Decomposition--integration frameworks built on complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) have advanced this field, yet existing CEEMDAN hybrids remain unstable: high-frequency mode mixing contaminates downstream predictors, and the heterogeneous volatility of decomposed components is not matched by any volatility-adaptive mechanism. To address these gaps, this paper proposes CEEMDAN-SE-SSA-VMGL. CEEMDAN adaptively decomposes the original series, and sample-entropy-driven K-means clustering groups the intrinsic mode functions into high-, mid-, and low-frequency and residual components. Singular spectrum analysis (SSA) is then applied to the high-frequency group, whose singular-value decomposition of the trajectory matrix and principal-component reconstruction decouple intertwined pseudo-oscillatory modes and alleviate mode mixing. We further design a plug-and-play robust LSTM variant, the volatility-modulated GARCH-LSTM (VMGL), which embeds a differentiable GARCH(1,1) layer inside the LSTM cell as a risk gate; the estimated conditional volatility multiplicatively scales the forget-gate activation, letting memory decay adapt to the instantaneous volatility while remaining fully end-to-end trainable. Component predictions are linearly combined. On five US and Chinese financial series (CSI 300, CSI 500, AAPL, GOOGL, S&P 500), the proposed model is compared with eleven benchmark architectures, lowering the forecasting error by 9% to 24% relative to the best benchmark and by 60% to 70% relative to a standard LSTM. Diebold--Mariano and model-confidence-set (MCS) tests confirm the statistical significance and stability of the gains, spectral analyses verify the SSA denoising efficacy, and backtesting demonstrates the framework's economic utility.
