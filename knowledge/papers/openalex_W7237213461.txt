Abstract
Accurate numerical treatment of the distributed-order time-fractional Black–Scholes 
model becomes especially diﬃcult when the solution lacks smoothness, as 
commonly occurs in option pricing applications. The distributed-order time-fractional 
Black–Scholes model (DO-TFBSM) extends the classical Black–Scholes equation by 
incorporating a continuous range of fractional orders, allowing it to capture memory 
eﬀects and anomalous temporal behavior more eﬀectively. This study presents a 
novel and eﬃcient spectral method for the numerical solution of the DO-TFBSM, 
particularly suited for problems characterized by non-smooth solutions and limited 
regularity. A new class of fractional shifted Legendre cardinal functions (FSLCFs) is 
introduced, along with a corresponding operational matrix for distributed-order 
Caputo derivatives, enabling precise and computationally eﬃcient treatment of 
memory eﬀects. Building on these components, a hybrid spectral tau scheme is 
formulated by coupling FSLCFs with standard shifted Legendre cardinal functions 
(SLCFs), ensuring spectral accuracy even in the presence of nonsmooth functions. 
A rigorous convergence analysis establishes the theoretical soundness of the 
proposed approach. Extensive numerical experiments further conﬁrm its accuracy, 
robustness, and eﬃciency, demonstrating its strong potential as a reliable 
computational framework for pricing ﬁnancial derivatives in markets governed by 
evolving memory and non-local temporal dynamics.
Keywords: Black–Scholes model; Distributed-order; Fractional shifted Legendre 
cardinal function; Convergence analysis 
1 Introduction
Once the stochastic process governing an asset’s price is speciﬁed, the valuation of deriva-
tive instruments naturally follows. Options are ﬁnancial contracts that grant the holder 
the right but not the obligation to buy or sell an underlying asset at a predetermined strike 
price, either on or before a speciﬁed expiration date [1]. They are commonly classiﬁed as 
European options, which may be exercised only at maturity, or American options, which 
can be exercised at any time prior to expiration; and further as calls (rights to buy) or puts 
(rights to sell) [2]. The classical Black–Scholes (BS) model, introduced in 1973, represents

less markets, constant volatility, and Markovian behavior. Although it yields a closed-form 
solution for European options, it fails to reproduce several empirically observed market 
features, including volatility clustering, heavy-tailed return distributions, and long-range 
temporal dependence [1, 2].
These deﬁciencies are quantitative as well as qualitative: implied volatility recovered 
from traded quotes forms a pronounced smile rather than the ﬂat surface predicted, and 
the autocorrelation of absolute returns decays as a power law, a signature of persistence 
that no memoryless description reproduces. Endowing the time derivative with a Caputo 
order ϑ supplies the kernel (ξ –γ )–ϑ and hence memory, yet a single ϑ imposes one decay 
exponent across all market regimes and investor horizons; weighting by ϱ(ϑ)superposes a

The cost is computational: nonlocality compounded by quadrature in ϑ, together with 
solutions behaving like tϑ at the origin and a payoﬀ kink at the strike, forfeits polynomial 
spectral accuracy and motivates the basis developed here.
Within this fractional framework, a substantial literature has developed [3, 4]. A semi-
nal contribution in this direction is the ﬁnite moment log-stable model proposed by Carr 
et al. [5], which has shown improved empirical consistency compared with classical for-
mulations. This framework has further inspired the development of Fourier-based op-
tion pricing methods [6]. Subsequently, Cartea et al. [7] established a rigorous connection 
between the ﬁnite moment log-stable model and spatial fractional derivatives, facilitat-
ing the formulation of fractional partial diﬀerential equations (FPDEs) for pricing ﬁnan-
cial derivatives. Building on this foundation, Chen et al. [8] derived closed-form expres-
sions for European option prices within the ﬁnite moment log-stable setting, oﬀering a 
more computationally eﬃcient alternative to transform based approaches. On the tem-
poral side, Wyss [9] introduced a time-fractional variant of the BS model, providing a 
closed-form solution that accounts for temporal memory characteristics. This formula-
tion was subsequently examined using Laplace transform techniques by Kumar et al. [10]. 
Supporting the practical relevance of fractional dynamics, Cartea [11] provided empirical 
evidence based on high-frequency trading data that option prices evolve according to a 
time FPDEs involving the Caputo derivative.
The evolution of fractional BS models has been marked by several key contributions. 
Jumarie [12, 13] developed space–time fractional variants incorporating Gaussian noise, 
capturing both memory eﬀects and spatial heterogeneities. Song et al. [14] focused on the 
numerical resolution of these models using ﬁnite diﬀerence methods. Motivated by analo-
gies with fractal transmission systems and earlier work on anomalous diﬀusion [15, 16], 
Liang et al. [17] proposed a bifractional BS model, where both temporal and spatial frac-
tional derivatives drive the dynamics, reﬂecting the multiscale complexity of ﬁnancial 
markets. Later extensions have introduced hybrid features, including jump diﬀusion pro-
cesses [18], stochastic volatility, and distributed-order memory eﬀects [19, 20], aiming 
to more realistically model the nonlocal and multiscale behavior observed in markets. 
In addition, Ariel et al. [21] studied European and American options under a pure time-
fractional framework, while Cen et al. [22] and Zhang et al. [23, 24] proposed tempered 
fractional models to address issues related to boundary conditions and inﬁnite activity 
processes.

Alongside these theoretical advances, signiﬁcant progress has been made in the numer-
ical treatment of FPDEs. Li et al. [25] developed ﬁnite diﬀerence schemes, while Yang et 
al. [26] employed spectral methods to enhance solution accuracy. Amini et al. [27] in-
troduced meshless approaches based on radial basis functions, oﬀering greater ﬂexibil-
ity for handling complex geometries and boundary conditions. Together, these methods 
have broadened the practical applicability of fractional models in computational ﬁnance. 
In the multidimensional setting, Chen et al. [28] formulated a two-dimensional space-
fractional BS model driven by independent geometric Lévy processes. Prathumwan et 
al. [29] addressed the two-dimensional time-fractional model for European options us-
ing the Laplace homotopy perturbation method, while Chen et al. [30] proposed a time-
fractional formulation incorporating a fractal transmission mechanism to better capture 
the heterogeneous structure of ﬁnancial systems. From an analytical perspective, vari-
ous techniques have been applied to FPDEs, including the fractional variational iteration 
method [31], the method of Lagrange multipliers [32], Adomian decomposition [33], and 
ﬁtted ﬁnite volume schemes [34]. Windcliﬀ et al. [35] examined the stability conditions 
of fractional models, and Barles et al. [36] studied utility-based valuation approaches for 
nonlinear BS equations. More recently, Xingyu et al. [37] estimated parameters of the 
time-fractional BS model using S&P 500 option data, demonstrating that the fractional 
order, identiﬁed via high order numerical techniques combined with hybrid optimization, 
serves as a robust indicator of market ﬂuctuations and that the TFBSM outperforms clas-
sical models under high volatility conditions.
Despite these signiﬁcant theoretical and numerical advancements, several obstacles 
continue to limit the practical adoption of fractional models in ﬁnance. In particular, 
many time-fractional formulations also incorporate spatial-fractional terms, often with-
out a clear ﬁnancial justiﬁcation [9, 10, 38]. Additionally, a widely accepted rationale for 
favoring time-fractional dynamics over classical models remains lacking. A major unre-
solved issue is the absence of a uniﬁed fractional framework capable of simultaneously 
capturing multiple market features, including stochastic volatility, regime switching be-
havior, and long-memory eﬀects. Moreover, the accurate and eﬃcient numerical solution 
of FPDEs especially in the presence of nonsmooth or path dependent payoﬀs remains an 
active and challenging area of research.

limitation, the distributed-order fractional framework has been proposed. This approach 
allows a continuum of fractional orders, weighted according to a distribution function, 
providing a more ﬂexible and accurate representation of complex memory structures. In 
the present study, we investigate a DO-TFBSM deﬁned on the spatial domain ζ ∈ (ζL,ζR)

This study introduces an innovative spectral technique for solving the DO-TFBSM, with 
a particular focus on accurately resolving solution behaviors exhibiting weak regularity 
and non-smooth characteristics. The major contributions of this work are outlined as fol-
lows:
• We construct a new family of FSLCFs in summation form which designed as an 
eﬃcient basis for function approximation, particularly suited for problems involving 
non-smooth solutions.
• An FSLCF based operational matrix is derived for the distributed-order Caputo 
derivative to enable computationally accurate evaluation of memory eﬀects.
• A hybrid SLCFs-FSLCFs space time spectral tau method is developed to allow a high 
accuracy even in the presence of low regularity in the solution.
• A convergence analysis is presented for the proposed hybrid method, establishing its 
theoretical robustness.
• Comprehensive numerical experiments are carried out to evaluate the performance of 
the method, demonstrating its accuracy and capability to capture intricate temporal 
memory eﬀects in nonsmooth ﬁnancial structures.
These developments provide a powerful and ﬂexible computational framework for op-
tion pricing in ﬁnancial markets characterized by evolving memory and non-local tempo-
ral dynamics.

7 Conclusion
The proposed fractional spectral technique based on FSLCFs oﬀers an accurate and eﬃ-
cient solution strategy for the DO-TFBSM. By embedding fractional orders into the basis, 
the method captures memory-dependent and nonsmooth behaviors that SLCFs fail to rep-
resent. The hybrid tau formulation combining FSLCFs and SLCFs achieves spectral accu-
racy and excellent performance across both standard and extended spatial domains. A de-
tailed convergence analysis establishes the theoretical soundness of the proposed scheme, 
ensuring exponential-type convergence for solutions with suﬃcient regularity and con-
ﬁrming its stability even when the computational domain is extended. These theoretical 
ﬁndings are reinforced by numerical experiments, which report exceptionally low maxi-
mum absolute errors and demonstrate the ability of the fractional basis to adapt eﬀectively 
to the distributed-order memory eﬀects inherent in the model.
