Abstract
High-frequency volatility forecasting typically separates measure-
ment from prediction: intraday observations are first compressed
into a daily realized measure using a fixed aggregation rule, and
the resulting statistic is then passed to a forecasting model. This
conventional pipeline therefore treats measure construction as
forecast-agnostic preprocessing. We ask whether the aggregation
stage itself can instead be learned from the downstream forecasting
objective while preserving the statistical structure of established
realized measures. We propose Neural Realized Measures (NRM), a
primitive-preserving, forecast-supervised aggregation framework
that retains the original measure-specific intraday contributions
but replaces uniform aggregation with positive, mean-normalized
context-dependent weights. Uniform weights exactly recover the
corresponding raw realized measure (RRM), so the classical con-
struction is nested as a special case. Because NRM remains an
explicit weighted sum of established primitive contributions, it also
retains direct bar-level attribution.
Across SF, HAR, and MIDAS forecasting specifications, NRM
is evaluated against corresponding RRM specifications under con-
trolled comparisons. In Heston simulations, NRM reduces RMSE
for both contemporaneous and next-day latent integrated variance
across all evaluated primitive–regime combinations. On six cryp-
tocurrencies using five-minute data, NRM improves QLIKE in all 54
paired out-of-sample comparisons, with mean gains of 7.23%, 8.15%,
and 9.22% under SF, HAR, and MIDAS, respectively. Validation-
selected NRM specifications also achieve the lowest mean QLIKE
across the six assets among the evaluated approaches. Ablation
and SHAP analyses reveal systematic time-of-day and nonlinear
local-context effects in the learned allocation.

realized volatility, volatility forecasting, neural aggregation, high-

The conventional pipeline nevertheless contains a hidden restric-
tion: the measurement stage is fixed before the forecasting objective
is specified. The primitive determines what each bar contributes,
but the aggregation rule determines how those contributions are
compressed into the daily statistic, and this rule remains forecast-
agnostic. In the classical construction, every primitive contribution
receives unit weight regardless of candlestick geometry, intraday
position, or magnitude relative to nearby bars. We refer to this
fixed-aggregation construction as the raw realized measure (RRM).
This motivates the central question of the paper: can the aggrega-
tion rule be made task-adaptive without discarding the statistical
primitive that defines the realized measure?
We address this question with Neural Realized Measures (NRM),
a primitive-preserving, forecast-supervised aggregation framework.

The paper makes four main contributions:
• Learned aggregation.Conventional realized measures use fixed
within-day aggregation rules. We instead treat the aggregation
step as part of the forecasting problem, allowing the importance
of intraday observations to be learned directly from the forecast-
ing objective.
• Primitive preservation.We introduce NRM as a general ag-
gregation framework for realized measures with primitive-level
additive representations. NRM reweights the underlying primi-
tive contributions using positive, mean-normalized, and context-
dependent weights. Uniform weighting recovers the correspond-
ing conventional realized measure exactly, so the original mea-
sure is nested as a special case of the proposed framework.
• Controlled evaluation.We compare each NRM directly with
its RRM counterpart while keeping the underlying primitive
and forecasting model unchanged. Across several primitive fam-
ilies and SF, HAR, and MIDAS specifications, simulations and
cryptocurrency data show that learned aggregation generally im-
proves both latent-volatility estimation and out-of-sample volatil-
ity forecasts.
• Interpretable allocation.Because NRM remains an explicit
weighted sum of the original primitive contributions, the learned
weights can be inspected directly. Ablation and SHAP analyses
show that the model systematically reallocates volatility infor-
mation according to time of day and nonlinear local market
conditions.
2 Related Literature and Positioning
NRM builds on realized-measure research that aggregates intra-
day returns, ranges, and OHLC information into daily variation
[1, 3, 4, 13, 21, 25]. Recent work also develops alternative high-
frequency volatility primitives and measurement rules that exploit
persistent return distortions [2], candlestick geometry [19], or, more
recently, the occupation structure of the intraday price path [ 6],
introducing a distinct path-based characterization of intraday vari-
ation. These approaches expand the information encoded in the
primitive itself. NRM studies a complementary design dimension:
it keeps a given primitive fixed and learns how its intraday contri-
butions are aggregated for forecasting.
HAR and MIDAS capture persistent volatility through hetero-
geneous or parameterized historical lags [8, 14]. In this paper they
serve as downstream forecasting heads: MIDAS aggregates informa-
tion across days, whereas NRM reallocates primitive contributions
within a day. Paired RRM–NRM comparisons therefore isolate the
effect of learned aggregation while holding the downstream fore-
casting specification fixed.
Neural volatility forecasting spans both structured extensions
of realized-volatility models and more direct mappings from high-
frequency inputs to forecasts. HARNet bridges the HAR structure
and dilated convolutional networks [24]; Zhang et al. [28] use past
intraday realized volatilities as neural predictors; and DeepVol maps
high-frequency information through a dilated causal-convolution
architecture [22]. NRM takes a complementary route by placing
neural learning at the realized-measure construction stage. It as-
signs context-dependent positive weights to predefined primitive
contributions while preserving an explicit daily realized measure.
NRM is also conceptually related to supervised dimension re-
duction and learned aggregation. Sufficient forecasting uses infor-
mation from the prediction target to construct low-dimensional
predictive indices [ 12], while attention-based multiple-instance
learning learns task-dependent weights for aggregating collections
of instances [17]. NRM shares the principle that aggregation can be
informed by the downstream task, but imposes a stronger structural
restriction: rather than pooling unrestricted learned embeddings, it
aggregates predefined, statistically interpretable realized-measure
primitives. Learning changes their relative allocation but not the
primitive being aggregated, and uniform weights exactly recover
the corresponding classical realized measure. NRM is therefore best
viewed as forecast-supervised statistical aggregation rather than a
generic attention architecture.
Recent time-series research emphasizes task-specific inductive
bias over architectural scale alone [11, 18, 26, 27]. NRM follows this
principle by placing model flexibility at a narrowly defined stage
of the pipeline: the aggregation of statistically grounded intraday
contributions. The objective is not to replace domain structure with
a larger architecture, but to identify which part of that structure
benefits from task-adaptive learning.

The protocol.
(1) fit each neural candidate on the training sample using QLIKE
and the KL-to-uniform penalty;
(2) early-stop each candidate using validation log-MSE; for each
candidate NRM representation, the corresponding forecasting-
head coefficients are re-estimated using training observations
only;
(3) select one common specification using mean validation MSE
on the variance scale across seeds 11, 22, and 33;
(4) retain the three independently trained fits of that selected spec-
ification, evaluate each once on the held-out test period, and
report test losses averaged across the seeds. No test observation
is used for fitting, specification selection, or seed selection.
The corresponding RRM specification uses the same forecasting and
evaluation setup but retains uniform intraday weights. This paired
design evaluates the incremental value of learned aggregation while
holding the downstream forecasting specification fixed; a separate
NRM is estimated for each forecasting specification.
Implementation and computational details.The aggregation weight
layer for NRM score in Eq (8) uses GELU activation, dropout 0.1,
and hidden dimensions selected from[8, 4] and[16, 8]. The valida-
tion grid considers 1-, 4-, 6-, and 12-hour centered same-day local
windows, corresponding to 12, 48, 72, and 144 five-minute bars. We
fix softmax temperature at𝜏= 1and select 𝜆 for the KL-to-uniform
strength from{3×10−4, 10−3, 3×10−3, 10−2, 3×10−2}; this regularizer
directly governs how strongly the fitted weights may depart from
the classical uniform allocation. Neural candidates are trained with
AdamW, batch size 16, a maximum of 200 epochs, and validation-

Experiments were executed on an Ubuntu 22.04 CPU server with

4.4 Model selection and evaluation
For every reported specification, the hyperparameter configuration
is selected by mean validation MSE averaged across repeats using
seeds 11, 22, and 33. This common criterion provides a consistent
selection rule and avoids using QLIKE for both parameter optimiza-
tion and configuration selection. For each selected configuration,
three independently trained fits are obtained using the same three
seeds. Each fitted model forecasts the full test sample, and test
RMSE, MAE, and QLIKE are averaged across the three repeats and
reported in Table 4, with performance summaries in Tables 2 and 3.
The primary comparison pairs each NRM specification with its
corresponding RRM specification while holding the downstream
forecasting specification fixed. This design evaluates the incremen-
tal value of learned aggregation; parameter counts need not be equal
because the NRM aggregation layer is omitted for RRM. QLIKE is
included in the comparison because it supports robust volatility-
forecast comparison when latent variance is evaluated using a noisy
proxy [23]. RMSE and MAE are also reported on the variance scale.

7 Conclusion
This paper studies whether forecast-supervised intraday realloca-
tion can improve volatility prediction while retaining the primitive-
level structure of conventional realized measures. The design treats
established realized-measure primitives as statistically grounded
building blocks and applies neural learning to the aggregation rule
rather than replacing those primitives. NRM preserves the chosen

Because the output remains an explicit weighted sum of the original

Controlled simulations show lower reported losses for contem-
poraneous and next-day latent integrated variance across the eval-
uated continuous, jump, and conditional event-stress regimes. In
cryptocurrency data, paired RRM–NRM comparisons show broad
gains under SF, HAR, and MIDAS forecasting specifications, with
particularly consistent improvements under QLIKE. Validation-
selected NRM specifications attain the lowest mean QLIKE across
the six assets among the evaluated approaches. For representa-
tive BTC, ETH, and SOL specifications, the fitted score functions
combine day-level distributional conditioning, recurring intraday
timing, and nonlinear local-context adjustments.
The evidence has several boundaries. NRM is forecasting-specification-
specific and should be interpreted as a task-specific representa-
tion rather than a reusable universal measure. The cryptocurrency
setting provides consistent 24/7 UTC days but does not establish
generalization to markets with sessions and overnight gaps. The
empirical target is a noisy realized proxy, the simulation regimes
are controlled stresses rather than fully calibrated microstructure
models, and STLG excludes richer volume and order-book informa-
tion. Future work can examine transferable aggregation, alternative
day boundaries, traditional asset classes, and economic-value appli-
cations.
