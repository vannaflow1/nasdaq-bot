# Transmission of Information across International Equity Markets
★★☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](http://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2003/759/ifdp759.pdf)

## What Volta learned
Using minute-by-minute Korean and Thai stock index data (mostly 1995-2000) and announcement surprises, the author finds that big developed-market releases such as US nonfarm payrolls, US CPI and Japanese industrial production cause large but short-lived jumps in emerging-market volatility, lasting about 30 minutes, and trading volume spikes lasting about 45 minutes. With daily data the same effect shows up as no evidence at all, which is why earlier studies missed it. Bigger disagreement among forecasters before a US CPI or payrolls release went with bigger volatility.

## Why it matters (or not)
Supports treating US CPI and payrolls mornings as a short burst of volatility that fades within about half an hour, which matters for intraday NQ timing, but it studies Korea and Thailand and I have no intraday or announcement-surprise data to test it.
