# The Impact of Market Maker Competition on Market Quality: Evidence from an Options Exchange
☆☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://www.uowoajournals.org/aabfj/article/id/1326/download/pdf/)

## What Volta learned
This study tracked market makers entering and leaving 134 Australian stock option classes from 2000 to 2006. Market makers crowd into the busiest names (about 8.5 per liquid class versus 1.8 per illiquid one) and decide to join or quit based on spreads, volume, volatility and how many rivals are already there, yet competition stays thin: the concentration score (Herfindahl) averaged 0.17 for liquid classes and 0.45 for illiquid ones, far above the level a fully competitive market would show. Adding a dealer improves things most in the liquid classes, so more market makers do not automatically mean better prices in thinly traded options.

## Why it matters (or not)
This is about exchange market-maker competition in Australian single-stock options in the early 2000s; it says nothing usable about NQ/QQQ moves or day-trading decisions, and QQQ options are among the most competitively quoted in the world.
