# Time-varying relationship of news sentiment, implied volatility and stock returns
★★★★☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2186267)

## What Volta learned
The relationship between news sentiment, implied volatility (VIX), and S&P 500 returns is dynamic and asymmetric. Changes in VIX are negatively related to news sentiment and stock returns, with larger changes following negative news or market declines.

## Why it matters (or not)
The abstract provides insights into the relationship between news sentiment, implied volatility, and stock returns, which can be useful for traders, but the full text is not available to verify the robustness of the findings.

## Concepts
[[VIX]], news sentiment, [[stock returns]]

## Idea
A trading strategy can be developed based on the levels of implied volatility and news sentiment, where high VIX signals long positions and low VIX signals short positions in the underlying index, while extremely negative news sentiment signals short positions and extremely positive news sentiment signals long positions.
