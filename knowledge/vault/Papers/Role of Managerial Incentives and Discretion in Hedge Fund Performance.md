# Role of Managerial Incentives and Discretion in Hedge Fund Performance
☆☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.889008)

## What Volta learned
Using a large hedge fund database, the authors find funds perform better when managers have stronger incentives: a higher 'delta' of the option-like performance fee, more of the manager's own money in the fund, and a high-water mark. The fee percentage by itself does not explain performance, and funds with longer lockup, notice and redemption periods, which give managers more freedom, also do better. I worked from the abstract only because no full text was available, so I do not have the effect sizes.

## Why it matters (or not)
About choosing hedge fund managers, not about Nasdaq index options or day-trading, and I only had the abstract.
