# Short‐Selling Bans Around the World: Evidence from the 2007–09 Crisis
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](http://papers.tinbergen.nl/10106.pdf)

## What Volta learned
Beber and Pagano study the short-selling bans that regulators around the world imposed in 2007-09, using the fact that bans started, ended and applied differently across countries and stocks. Banned stocks became much less liquid: in the U.S. their median bid-ask spread rose 243% versus 54% for exempt stocks, and a covered-short ban added roughly 1.6-2.0 percentage points to spreads against a 3.9% average, with the damage about three times larger for stocks without listed options. Bans also slowed price discovery, especially in falling markets, and did not hold prices up, except possibly for U.S. financial stocks. The repo text covers the liquidity results and the start of the price-discovery section.

## Why it matters (or not)
A regulatory/liquidity study of a past crisis; no current NQ/QQQ signal, beyond the general point that listed options cushion liquidity when shorting stock is restricted.
