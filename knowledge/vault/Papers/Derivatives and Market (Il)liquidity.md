# Derivatives and Market (Il)liquidity
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E157483FF813393044E8FF1B77BFF789/S0022109023000224a.pdf/div-class-title-derivatives-and-market-il-liquidity-div.pdf)

## What Volta learned
Huang, Yueshen and Zhang (JFQA 2023) build a theory model of how options and other non-linear derivatives change liquidity in the underlying stock or index. Informed investors who expect calm markets sell derivatives, and when buyers and sellers delta-hedge, the hedging trades dilute informed trading, so each trade moves the price less (liquidity looks better). Whether prices bounce back after moves depends on the derivatives' gamma, and the model warns that usual illiquidity measures may not line up with the liquidity risk premium once derivatives trade. It is pure theory with no numbers, and only the abstract was available.

## Why it matters (or not)
A theory paper (worked from the abstract only, the saved text was the journal page) that supports the general idea that dealer gamma shapes whether moves reverse, but it gives no concrete, testable numbers for NQ/QQQ.
