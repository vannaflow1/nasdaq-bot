# Why Do Markets Move Together? An Investigation of U.S.‐Japan Stock Return Comovements
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://doi.org/10.7916/d87p95w7)

## What Volta learned
Using 1988-1992 transaction data on Japanese stocks traded as ADRs in New York and a matched set of US stocks, the authors ask what makes US and Japanese returns move together. US macro announcements, yen/dollar shocks, T-bill moves and industry effects had no measurable effect on the correlation; only large shocks to the broad indexes (Nikkei and S&P 500) raised both how strongly and how long the two markets moved together. I worked from the abstract only because no full text was available, so I do not have the size of the effect.

## Why it matters (or not)
Says big index shocks make markets move together more, which is a general caution for overnight risk, but it is 1990s US-Japan stock data with no Nasdaq options angle, and I only had the abstract.
