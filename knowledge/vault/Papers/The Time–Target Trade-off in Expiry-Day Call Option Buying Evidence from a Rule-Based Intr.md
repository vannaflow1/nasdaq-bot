# The Time–Target Trade-off in Expiry-Day Call Option Buying: Evidence from a Rule-Based Intraday Strategy in the Indian Index Derivatives Market
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijrsi/uploads/vol13-iss8-pg4203-4207-202609_pdf.pdf)

## What Volta learned
Two Indian researchers logged 24 expiry-day trades buying index call options around 2:00 PM and selling later that afternoon: 14 made money, 9 lost and 1 was flat, with a median gain of about 3.7% but a range from -95% to +77% of the premium paid. The average trade lasted about 25 minutes, and longer holds tended to do worse (correlation about -0.55 between holding time and return), while higher India VIX did not help (correlation about -0.31). The authors suggest smaller profit targets and short holds, but admit the sample is tiny.

## Why it matters (or not)
Expiry-day call buying is close to what 0DTE QQQ traders do, but 24 trades from one Indian market with no control group is far too little evidence to act on; mainly a reminder that late-day 0DTE buying can lose almost the whole premium.
