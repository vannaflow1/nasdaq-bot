# Betting against beta
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X13002675/pdf)

## What Volta learned
Frazzini and Pedersen argue that many investors cannot borrow, so they buy high-beta (extra-jumpy) assets to get more market exposure, which bids those assets up and leaves them with lower risk-adjusted returns. A 'betting against beta' portfolio, long leveraged low-beta assets and short high-beta assets, earned significant positive risk-adjusted returns in US stocks, 20 international stock markets, Treasuries, corporate bonds and futures. It does badly when funding conditions tighten, and tighter funding pushes betas toward one. Worked from the abstract only (no full text in the repo), so no return figures are given.

## Why it matters (or not)
A cross-sectional portfolio factor that needs many assets; it says little about next-day NQ/QQQ moves or options and cannot be tested on Volta's single-index memory.
