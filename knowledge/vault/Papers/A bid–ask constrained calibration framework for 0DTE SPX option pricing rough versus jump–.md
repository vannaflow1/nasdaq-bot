# A bid–ask constrained calibration framework for 0DTE SPX option pricing rough versus jump–diffusion volatility models
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050700626000824/pdf)

## What Volta learned
The authors fit four 'rough volatility' models and one simpler jump model to intraday SPX same-day (0DTE) volatility smiles over five days, counting a fit as good only if model prices land inside the bid-ask spread. One rough model (QRH+) fit best on days with a sharply curved downside smile, but the simpler jump-diffusion model matched or beat every rough model on the other days and on average. The takeaway is that fancy rough-volatility models are not required to price 0DTE options well.

## Why it matters (or not)
Worked from the abstract only (no full text available); a model-calibration study on just five SPX days, of little practical use for NQ/QQQ trading decisions.
