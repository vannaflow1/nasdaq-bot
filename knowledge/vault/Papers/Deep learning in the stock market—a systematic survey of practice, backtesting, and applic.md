# Deep learning in the stock market—a systematic survey of practice, backtesting, and applications
★☆☆☆☆  ·  OpenAlex (classic)  ·  [source](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10462-022-10226-0.pdf)

## What Volta learned
This survey reviews deep-learning research on stock markets, keeping only studies that actually backtest their models so a practitioner could judge real-world use. The qualifying work falls into seven areas, mostly trading strategy, price prediction and portfolio management, with few studies on market simulation, stock selection, hedging or risk management; returns and volatility are the metrics that matter most for judging these models. The authors find reproducibility has improved but explainability is still weak, and say longer-horizon prediction remains a big unsolved problem. Worked from the abstract only (no full text in the repo), so no counts or performance figures are given.

## Why it matters (or not)
A literature map, not a result: it offers no specific signal for NQ/QQQ options, only the general reminder to judge models by honest out-of-sample backtests, which idea_lab already does.
